# Stripe activation — what is asked, and where each answer comes from

The point of this file is that activation should be twenty minutes of typing,
not an afternoon of hunting. Everything Stripe asks that this project can
answer is answered here. Everything it asks that only the owner can answer is
named and deliberately **left blank** — this repository is public.

**Prerequisite: the site is live.** Activation asks for a business website and
a consumer AI product with no URL invites a manual review, which is why the
deploy moved ahead of this (CLAUDE.md §45A). Do `docs/deploy-runbook.md`
§§1–4 first.

---

## 0. Start a NEW account, not the sandbox

The account the test Prices live in is `ClearCost sandbox` — a different
product, and a sandbox cannot be verified at all (`details_submitted: false`,
no capabilities). Stripe Checkout prints the **account's** business name with
no per-Price override, which is why one account cannot serve two product
names.

So: `dashboard.stripe.com` → account switcher → **create a new account** for
Timestamp, then activate that one.

## 1. What only you can answer

Not in this file and not in this repository, on purpose (CLAUDE.md §42D):

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| Business type | Individual / sole trader (Einzelunternehmer) |
| Legal name | As on the ID document |
| Home address | This becomes the § 5 DDG disclosure address too |
| Date of birth | |
| Tax number / VAT ID | VAT ID only if you have one; `vatId: null` is valid |
| IBAN for payouts | |
| ID document | Passport or Personalausweis |

**Capture the legal name and address as you type them.** The same values go
into `TIMESTAMP_LEGAL_ENTITY` on the server, and **they must match what Stripe
shows on checkout** — a payment page naming one entity and an Impressum naming
another is the kind of mismatch that costs a customer's trust at the worst
moment.

## 3. The product description

Stripe's reviewer reads this, and for anything that generates imagery of real
people the description is what separates a legitimate service from the thing
they are actually screening for. Be specific and lead with consent.

> Timestamp sells short nostalgic videos. A customer uploads a photograph of
> themselves, chooses a setting and an outfit, and receives a fifteen-second
> video styled to look like a 2003 camcorder tape. Videos are generated by a
> third-party AI model and are marked as AI-generated in the file metadata and
> on the page where they are delivered.
>
> Customers may only upload a photograph of themselves, or of an adult who has
> given permission, and must confirm this before every order. Uploaded
> photographs are deleted after 7 days and finished videos after 30, and either
> can be deleted sooner on request. There is a published takedown route for
> anyone who appears in a video without having agreed.
>
> Payment is for credits, bought as a one-off bundle through Stripe Checkout.
> Credits are spent on videos. No card details ever reach our servers.

Every claim there is enforced in the product rather than aspirational — the
consent gate, the retention sweep, the metadata tag, the delivery page line.
If any of it stops being true, this paragraph has to change with it.

## 4. Two things that reach customers and are awkward to change

**Public business name.** Printed on the hosted payment page. Must match
`TIMESTAMP_LEGAL_ENTITY`. For a sole trader this is usually the legal name;
a trading name is fine where Stripe allows one, provided the Impressum still
names the person.

**Statement descriptor.** What appears on the card statement weeks later. An
unrecognisable descriptor is what a chargeback is made of, so it wants to be
the thing they would recognise — `TIMESTAMPTAPES` reads better than a legal
name nobody remembers giving money to.

## 5. After approval — the code half

Activation does not make anything buyable. In order:

1. **Create both Prices again on the live account.** A Price is immutable and
   lives in one mode on one account, so the two ids in `config/credits.json`
   cannot be promoted — their own `source` fields say `MUST NOT GO LIVE`.
   Starter **$12 / 92 credits**, Standard **$19 / 138 credits**, both
   **one-time** (a recurring Price is refused by Stripe at the moment a
   customer clicks Buy, in front of them, not at deploy).
2. **Paste the live ids** into `config/credits.json`, commit, deploy.
3. **`STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`** on the server becomes the live key.
4. **A new webhook endpoint** on the live account →
   `https://timestamptapes.com/api/stripe/webhook`, event
   `checkout.session.completed`; its signing secret becomes
   `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.
5. **Only a live-mode event grants credits** — that is deliberate (CLAUDE.md
   §28 item 1), so a test event against the live endpoint moves nothing and
   that is not a fault.
6. **Buy one pack with a real card and refund it.** The whole path has been
   proven in test mode against genuinely-signed events; it has never been
   proven live, and the first person to find out should not be a customer.

## 6. If review comes back asking questions

Most likely, in order: they want to see the website working (it is — give
them the URL); they want the refund policy stated (it is — `/terms`); or they
ask how consent for the uploaded likeness is obtained (the consent gate at
signup and at every order, plus the takedown route, all in `/terms`).
